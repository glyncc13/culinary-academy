# Worked lesson packs — draft
## Level 1: Weighing and recipe scaling
Objectives: distinguish mass and volume; scale a recipe; verify portion yield.
Equipment: digital scales, bowls, recipe card, calculator.
Method: read the original yield; divide target portions by original portions; multiply each ingredient by that factor; tare the bowl before weighing each ingredient; record actual weights; review seasoning and cooking time separately rather than assuming they scale linearly.
Example: a four-portion recipe uses 400 g vegetables, 200 g rice and 600 ml stock. For six portions multiply by 1.5: 600 g, 300 g and 900 ml. Follow the recipe's cooking and safety controls.
Practical: scale a recipe to six portions and weigh each ingredient. Supervisor checks calculations, taring, accuracy, organisation and clean-down.
Reflection: what would change if the pan capacity were exceeded?
## Level 2: Roux and thickened sauces
Objectives: explain starch thickening; produce a smooth sauce; diagnose lumps and excessive thickness.
Equipment: saucepan, whisk, scales, heatproof jug. Check allergens including wheat and milk.
Method: weigh ingredients using an approved recipe; melt fat gently; stir in flour; cook while stirring without burning; gradually incorporate liquid while whisking; simmer according to recipe until cooked and smooth; assess coating consistency; adjust with suitable liquid if too thick; serve or hold/cool under workplace controls.
Practical: produce two small batches with different flour quantities and compare consistency at the same temperature. Record recipe, observations, allergen controls and waste.
Reflection: explain how temperature, starch quantity and agitation affected the result.
## Level 3: Gross profit and pricing
Objectives: distinguish margin and markup; calculate portion cost from usable yield; calculate net selling price.
Method: convert purchase units to recipe units; divide purchase cost by usable quantity; total all recipe ingredients including garnish; divide by saleable portions; calculate gross profit as net sales less ingredient cost; divide gross profit by net sales for margin. Target net price = portion cost / (1 - target margin).
Example: £3 ingredient cost at 70% target margin requires £10 net selling price. If standard-rate VAT applies, £12 gross at 20%; confirm tax treatment. Labour and overhead are not included in this ingredient margin.
Practical: cost a fictional five-dish menu, calculate target prices, compare actual yields and explain one cost improvement without compromising safety or quality.
Reflection: why can a high-margin dish still be commercially unsuccessful?
## Practical marking framework
For each task assess safety (mandatory gate), preparation, technique, quality, organisation and reflection. Rate each non-safety criterion 0 not demonstrated, 1 developing, 2 competent, 3 consistent. Proposed practical threshold: at least 2 on every criterion and no critical safety failure. Supervisor records observations and corrective actions; reassessment after remediation. This is a proposed internal rubric, not an implemented certificate condition.
